# Reranking in RAG Retrieval Pipelines

Reranking is a crucial component in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, enhancing the relevance and quality of retrieved documents before they are fed into large language models (LLMs). This document synthesizes the current understanding of how reranking works, its advantages, examples of rerankers used in RAG systems, implementation strategies, and its impact on response relevance.

## What is Reranking?

Reranking refers to the process of reordering documents retrieved by an initial retrieval step based on their relevance to a given query. The objective is to improve the quality of document selection by employing more sophisticated scoring mechanisms than those used in basic retrieval methods (e.g., cosine similarity).

### Why Reranking Matters
- **Noise Reduction**: Initial retrieval often returns numerous documents, many of which may be irrelevant or noisy. Reranking helps filter these out, allowing for a better selection of documents to base LLM outputs on.
- **Improved Accuracy**: By using more nuanced scoring metrics, such as those based on deeper relevance or context, reranking can significantly enhance the accuracy of generated responses and reduce the likelihood of hallucinations.
- **Resource Efficiency**: Reranking helps in saving computational resources by ensuring that only the top-quality documents are processed in the generation step, reducing the amount of data the LLM needs to evaluate.

## Advantages of Reranking
- **Higher Response Quality**: Reranking can lead to more accurate and context-aware responses from LLMs. For instance, in a legal RAG, prioritizing documents relevant to case law can dramatically increase the accuracy of advice generated.
- **Scalability Improvements**: It allows RAG systems to handle larger corpora efficiently without sacrificing the quality of the outputs. This is crucial for maintaining performance as datasets grow.
- **Reduction of Hallucination**: Reranking reduces instances where LLMs generate incorrect or misleading information based on irrelevant documents.

## Examples of Rerankers in RAG Systems
1. **BM25**: A traditional information retrieval model that ranks documents based on term frequency and inverse document frequency. It is effective for keyword-heavy queries.
2. **Cross Encoder**: Utilizes a model to evaluate pairs of documents and queries directly, offering refined relevance scores based on deeper contextual understanding.
3. **Cohere Rerank**: A more advanced scoring mechanism designed to improve the quality of the document selection process, often using natural language understanding capabilities.
4. **Flash Rerank**: A method that aims for rapid processing and scoring, ideally suited for applications requiring quick response times.

## Implementation Strategies
- **Two-Step Retrieval**: A common strategy involves performing an initial vector search followed by a reranking step. This approach combines the efficiency of approximate nearest neighbor searches with the precision of detailed reranking evaluations.
- **Hybrid Models**: Combining semantic (e.g., embeddings) and lexical (e.g., BM25) approaches ensures that both deep contextual relevance and keyword matching are considered in the final output.

## Impact on Response Relevance
Reranking significantly enhances the overall relevance of responses generated by RAG systems. For example, studies have shown that reranking can boost response accuracy from around 70% to 95% in specific use cases by ensuring that only the most pertinent documents inform the LLM's output.

## Summary
Reranking is an essential process in RAG systems that bridges the gap between retrieval and generation phases. By implementing effective reranking strategies, developers can dramatically improve the accuracy, relevance, and efficiency of LLM outputs.
